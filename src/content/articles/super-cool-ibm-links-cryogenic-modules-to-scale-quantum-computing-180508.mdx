> **Key Takeaways**
> - **Cryo is the scaling wall:** IBM's new [cryogenic modules (June 2025)](https://research.ibm.com/blog/quantum-cryogenic-infrastructure) link dilution fridges at 15mK to host 100K+ qubits for [Starling (2029)](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/roadmap) — the bottleneck was refrigeration, not gates, per [IBM Research](https://research.ibm.com/blog/quantum-roadmap-starling).
> - **Why linking matters:** One fridge caps ~5K qubits; Starling needs 200 logical qubits (~100K physical with [surface code](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.0928)) — linking is the only path without new physics, per [Nature 2024 on modular quantum](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07320-3).
> - **What to watch:** Coherence at scale, not qubit count. Track [IBM's Eagle → Condor → Starling fidelity](https://quantum.ibm.com/) and [QEC overhead](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.0928) — not press releases.

Markets move on narratives, but infrastructure moves on constraints. IBM's linked cryogenic modules — dilution fridges stitched into one mK environment — mark a step toward scaling quantum computers from hundreds to hundreds of thousands of qubits as it pursues fault-tolerant [Quantum Starling by 2029](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/roadmap). The story is less about "more qubits" than about refrigeration as infrastructure.

## The hype discount — why cryo decides

Every emerging category carries a hype premium that distorts expectation. For quantum, the premium is qubit count without context. The disciplined metric is *logical qubits at useful fidelity*.

A logical qubit per [surface code (Fowler et al. 2012)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.0928) needs ~500 physical qubits today. Starling's target of 200 logical qubits implies ~100K physical — far beyond one fridge's ~5K cap per [IBM Research June 2025](https://research.ibm.com/blog/quantum-cryogenic-infrastructure). Linking fridges at 15mK is not a nice-to-have; it's the road.

<Callout type="info" title="The 15mK number in context">
  Superconducting qubits live at 15 milliKelvin — colder than outer space — to preserve coherence per [Kjaergaard et al., Annual Review 2020](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-conmatphys-031119-050605). Every cable crossing a thermal stage leaks heat. Linking modules *without* breaking that budget is the engineering win IBM claims in [its June 2025 blog](https://research.ibm.com/blog/quantum-cryogenic-infrastructure).
</Callout>

## The incumbent's dilemma — build, buy, or wait

Established players face a choice when emerging tech arrives: adopt and cannibalize, or ignore and risk irrelevance. For IBM vs [Google Quantum AI](https://quantumai.google/) vs [Intel](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/research/quantum-computing.html), the window is the [Quantum Roadmap](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/roadmap): Loon (2025) tests linking, Starling (2029) needs it. The bet is separate P&L, not a line item.

## What would have to be true

To take Starling seriously, three preconditions must hold per [Nature 2024 on modular scaling](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07320-3):

1. **Link fidelity holds at 15mK.** Inter-module gates must not add dephasing — IBM's claim to watch in 2025 Loon data.
2. **QEC overhead falls.** Surface code 500:1 must improve via [qLDPC codes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02930) to make 100K tractable.
3. **A problem worth 200 logical qubits exists at paying-customer cost.** Per [McKinsey Quantum 2024](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-digital/our-insights/quantum-computing-funding-remains-strong-but-talent-gap-raises-concern), enterprise advantage today is still classical + quantum hybrid.

<Callout type="warning" title="The valley">
  Between 'interesting' and 'infrastructure' lies boring integration — standards ([IEEE Quantum](https://quantum.ieee.org/)), tooling ([Qiskit](https://qiskit.org/)), and training. That's where Starling lives until 2029. The link is necessary, not sufficient.
</Callout>

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why link fridges instead of building one big fridge?

Heat budget. A single dilution stage leaks ~1µW at 15mK per [Kjaergaard et al.](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-conmatphys-031119-050605). Linking keeps each stage near budget; one giant fridge would need new materials. Modular is the [Nature-approved path](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07320-3).

### Is 200 logical qubits enough?

For early fault tolerance, yes — enough for [quantum chemistry and optimization demos](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/roadmap) per IBM, not yet for Shor at RSA-2048 scale which needs ~4000 logical per [Fowler et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.0928).

### How does this compare to Google/Intel?

Google's [Sycamore roadmap](https://quantumai.google/) bets on single-chip scale; Intel on [silicon spin qubits](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/research/quantum-computing.html) at higher temp. IBM's link is the *cryo-modular* bet — distinct, testable by Loon fidelity data.
