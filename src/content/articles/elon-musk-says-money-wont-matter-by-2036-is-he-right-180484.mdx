> **Key Takeaways**
> - **What Musk actually said:** At [VivaTech 2024](https://vivatechnology.com/) and later in 2025, Musk argued AI + [Optimus](https://www.tesla.com/optimus) could create "universal high income" where "money becomes optional" by ~2036 — coverage via [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/musk-says-ai-will-make-jobs-optional/) and [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/elon-musk-ai-jobs-money-optional/).
> - **What the data says:** [IMF (Jan 2024)](https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2024/01/14/ai-will-transform-the-global-economy) estimates 40% of global jobs exposed, 60% in advanced economies; [McKinsey Global Institute 2023](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/mckinsey%20digital/our%20insights/generative-ai-could-enable-labor-productivity-growth-of-0-5-to-0-9-percent-a-year/generative-ai-could-enable-labor-productivity-growth.pdf) models net productivity gain 0.5–0.9pp/year — transformative, not post-scarcity by 2036.
> - **Scarcity that remains:** Land, energy, and human attention stay scarce even if labor automates — the constraint per [BLS productivity data](https://www.bls.gov/productivity/) and [IEA energy outlook](https://www.iea.org/reports/world-energy-outlook-2024). Money prices scarcity; abundance in one input doesn't erase the others.

Every technology story is really two stories: the thing that shipped, and the assumption it exposed about how the people building it think. Elon Musk's moneyless-future claim belongs to the second: it's less a prediction about currency than a bet about *what happens when labor is no longer the scarce input*.

We test that bet against the mechanism — not the persona.

## What Musk argued

Musk's framing, per [Reuters coverage of VivaTech 2024](https://www.reuters.com/technology/musk-says-ai-will-make-jobs-optional/) and his 2025 interviews summarized by [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/elon-musk-ai-jobs-money-optional/), is: AI + humanoid robots ([Optimus](https://www.tesla.com/optimus), now in limited factory pilots per [Tesla Q2 2024 update](https://ir.tesla.com/)) automate physical and cognitive labor, driving marginal cost toward zero, enabling "universal high income" where work is optional and money is less relevant by ~2036.

The antecedent is not new — [Keynes' 1930 "Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren"](https://www.econ.yale.edu/smith/econ116a/keynes1.pdf) predicted 15-hour work weeks — but Musk's timeline is specific and his mechanism (embodied AI) is testable.

![Abundance vs scarcity — what automates and what doesn't](/article-elon-musk-says-money-wont-matter-by-2036-is-he-right-180484.jpg)

<Callout type="info" title="The Keynes test">
  Keynes predicted abundance in 2030 from productivity. We got richer, but not post-money. [BLS data](https://www.bls.gov/productivity/) shows productivity doubled since 1970, yet hours fell only ~5% — because wants expand. That's the Jevons paradox we unpack in [our token-cost analysis](/articles/ai-costs-continue-to-rise-despite-falling-token-prices-180461).
</Callout>

## The three scarcities that money prices

Money doesn't just price labor. It prices *any* scarce input:

| Scarcity | Does AI/Optimus erase it by 2036? | Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Labor (cognitive + physical)** | Partially — [IMF 2024](https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2024/01/14/ai-will-transform-the-global-economy): 40% jobs exposed, but tasks re-bundle | [BLS task data](https://www.bls.gov/productivity/), [McKinsey 2023](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/mckinsey%20digital/our%20insights/generative-ai-could-enable-labor-productivity-growth-of-0-5-to-0-9-percent-a-year/generative-ai-could-enable-labor-productivity-growth.pdf) 0.5-0.9pp/yr |
| **Energy + land + materials** | No — [IEA 2024](https://www.iea.org/reports/world-energy-outlook-2024) sees data-center demand doubling; Optimus itself consumes energy and rare earths | [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/world-energy-outlook-2024), [USGS Mineral Commodities](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/national-minerals-information-center) |
| **Human attention + trust** | No — attention is rivalrous, per [Herbert Simon (1971)](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-95217-9_12) and our [trust-gap data](/articles/ai-trust-gap-with-young-adults-could-carry-a-high-cost-180511) | [Pew 2024 on AI concern](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2024/02/15/americans-and-ai/) 52% concerned |

Abundance in labor alone doesn't make money optional — you still need a coordination mechanism for what *remains* scarce.

## Why 2036 is too early for "optional"

Three constraints from the data:

1. **Productivity ≠ abundance overnight.** [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/mckinsey%20digital/our%20insights/generative-ai-could-enable-labor-productivity-growth-of-0-5-to-0-9-percent-a-year/generative-ai-could-enable-labor-productivity-growth.pdf) models AI adding 0.5–0.9 percentage points to annual productivity growth through 2060 — massive cumulatively, but a decade is one business cycle, not a phase change.
2. **Deployment lags capability.** Our [CTO scaling piece](/articles/cto-confidence-in-scaling-ai-falls-for-third-straight-year-180411) documents the gap: capability demos ship before accountability, eval harnesses, and unit economics do. Optimus factory pilots per [Tesla IR](https://ir.tesla.com/) are step one of a long operational tail.
3. **Distribution is political, not just technical.** Universal high income requires a transfer mechanism. [IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2024/01/14/ai-will-transform-the-global-economy) flags that without policy, AI widens inequality — money becomes *more* salient as a distribution tool, not less.

<Callout type="warning" title="The bet to watch, not the date">
  Track [Optimus mean time between interventions](https://www.tesla.com/optimus) in real factories and [BLS output per hour](https://www.bls.gov/productivity/) — not keynotes. When a robot does a full shift with <5% human assist *and* productivity inflects, the debate moves from 2036 to 2046.
</Callout>

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Did Musk say money will disappear in 2036?

He said money may become "optional" or less relevant if AI/robotics create abundance, per [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/musk-says-ai-will-make-jobs-optional/) and [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/elon-musk-ai-jobs-money-optional/). It's a vision, not a dated product commitment.

### Does IMF agree 40% of jobs are at risk?

[IMF (Jan 2024)](https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2024/01/14/ai-will-transform-the-global-economy) estimates 40% globally exposed, ~60% in advanced economies — with complementarity for many roles, not pure replacement. [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/mckinsey%20digital/our%20insights/generative-ai-could-enable-labor-productivity-growth-of-0-5-to-0-9-percent-a-year/generative-ai-could-enable-labor-productivity-growth.pdf) sees net productivity gain, not net job disappearance.

### What would make money truly optional?

Post-scarcity in *all* inputs — labor, energy, land, attention — per [Keynes](https://www.econ.yale.edu/smith/econ116a/keynes1.pdf) and [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/world-energy-outlook-2024). Labor alone is insufficient; coordination still needs pricing.
