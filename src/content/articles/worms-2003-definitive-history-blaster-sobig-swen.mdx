## Why these 17 reports belong together

Our newsroom filed 17 dispatches between July and September 2003 that, read separately, look like isolated outbreaks. Read together, they describe a single attack surface:

- **Windows RPC DCOM (MS03-026)** — the Blaster vector, scanned at scale before weaponization per [our scan report](/articles/windows-vulnerability-scans-increase-worm-likely-to-follow-31296) and confirmed when [Blaster spread](/articles/windows-threat-realized-blaster-worm-spreads-31315).
- **Email trust** — Sobig.F's address spoofing and open-relay abuse, profiled in [Superworm Sobig.E exposed](/articles/profile-of-the-superworm-sobig-e-exposed-31321), [Sobig piles on](/articles/sobig-f-piles-on-the-worm-effect-31408), and [fastest outbreak ever](/articles/spreading-sobig-f-variant-fastest-outbreak-ever-31393).
- **Social engineering at scale** — Swen's fake patch emails that infected 1.5M machines per [our Swen report](/articles/swen-worm-infects-over-1-5-million-computers-31632) and continued per [FBI/EPA and Blaster/Sobig tracking](/articles/feds-nab-blaster-suspect-continue-tracking-sobig-f-writer-31462).

| Worm | Vector | Authentication failure | Why it scaled | 2003 dispatch |

<Callout type="warning" title="The 2003 mistake that repeats">
  **Treating the perimeter as the trust boundary.** Blaster didn't need to phish; the vulnerable service was reachable. Sobig didn't need an exploit; the user *was* the exploit. Both are the same architectural failure — no segmentation, no least privilege. [NIST CSF PR.AC-4 and PR.DS-2](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework) exist because of this month.
</Callout>

## What the 17 dispatches got right (and what we know now)

- **Right: scans predict worms.** [Our July 31 warning](/articles/windows-vulnerability-scans-increase-worm-likely-to-follow-31296) preceded Blaster by 11 days — the same signal [CISA's KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog) now formalizes.
- **Right: tools circulate before the next variant.** [Aug 13 "attack tools circulate"](/articles/windows-attack-tools-circulate-another-virus-feared-31603) predicted Blaster's variants, later confirmed in [Suspect arrested](/articles/blaster-variant-suspect-arrested-31699).
- **Missed depth (understandable in 2003):** The organized-crime angle per [our inside story](/articles/computer-viruses-and-organized-crime-the-inside-story-31679) was early; the full commoditization came later and is now tracked via [Symantec Threat Intelligence](https://symantec-enterprise-blogs.security.com/blogs/threat-intelligence) and [CISA Alerts](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts).

## The patch lesson that still decides 2026 incidents

Three controls would have neutered 2003 and still neuter 2026:

1. **Patch within the exposure window.** MS03-026 was available July 16; Blaster hit August 11 — a 26-day window. [CISA's BOD 22-01](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/directives/bod-22-01) now mandates 15 days for KEVs. The gap is policy, not technology.
2. **Segment and disable by default.** RPC should never have been internet-reachable. This is [NIST CSF PR.AC-4](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework) and the core of zero-trust per [NIST SP 800-207](https://csrc.nist.gov/publications/detail/sp/800-207/final).
3. **Treat email attachments as code.** Sobig/Swen succeeded because `qpatch.exe` looked plausible. Code signing + attachment sandboxing per [CISA's phishing guidance](https://www.cisa.gov/topics/cybersecurity-best-practices) closes it. For the enterprise cost lens, see our [token-cost and verification analysis](/articles/ai-costs-continue-to-rise-despite-falling-token-prices-180461) — verification overhead is the same problem in a new costume.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why consolidate 17 articles into one pillar?

Because they describe one attack surface (unpatched edge + trusted email) exploited by three worms. Consolidation gives [CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog) and [NIST CSF](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework) a single canonical URL to cite, and prevents 17 thin pages from competing for "2003 worm" intent.

### What was the single biggest failure in 2003?

The 26-day patch window (MS03-026 July 16 → Blaster Aug 11) with no enforced deadline. [BOD 22-01](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/directives/bod-22-01) now mandates 15 days for KEVs — a policy fix for the same failure.

### How does this relate to zero-trust today?

Directly. 2003's flat network with reachable RPC is the anti-pattern [NIST SP 800-207](https://csrc.nist.gov/publications/detail/sp/800-207/final) was written to kill. Segment, least privilege, continuous verification — the pillar's three controls.
