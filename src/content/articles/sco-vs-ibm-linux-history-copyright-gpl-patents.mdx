> **Key Takeaways**
> - **One case, three legal theories:** SCO claimed (1) copyright in Unix flowed into Linux, (2) GPL was invalid, and (3) IBM breached Unix licenses — all tested in [our contemporaneous coverage](/articles/sco-claims-copyright-trumps-gpl-31357) and summarized in [SCO's evidence raises questions](/articles/scos-evidence-raises-questions-about-case-31386).
> - **IBM's patent counterclaim changed the math:** Big Blue's portfolio turned a one-way suit into mutual assured litigation per [our counterclaim report](/articles/big-blue-hits-sco-with-patent-counterclaim-31287) — a textbook deterrence pattern still taught in [Harvard Law's IP strategy](https://hls.harvard.edu/).
> - **What endured:** [GPLv2's enforceability](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/old-licenses/gpl-2.0.html) survived, the [Open Invention Network](https://www.openinventionnetwork.com/) was born from this era, and enterprises now price [license + patent risk](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/resources/open-source-guides) per [Linux Foundation guidance](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/resources/open-source-guides).

Between 2003 and 2004, The SCO Group sued IBM for $1B+ and threatened Linux users with licensing demands, arguing that Unix intellectual property had been copied into the Linux kernel. This pillar consolidates our 12 contemporaneous reports — from [SCO takes licensing fight further](/articles/sco-takes-linux-licensing-fight-further-31166) to [SGI removes code, rebuffs SCO](/articles/sgi-removes-code-rebuffs-sco-31769) — and adds the modern lens from [GNU GPL](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/old-licenses/gpl-2.0.html), [IANAL/Heise](https://www.heise.de/), [Harvard Law](https://hls.harvard.edu/), and [Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/resources/open-source-guides).

## The claim stack — what SCO actually argued

SCO's public position shifted, but three theories recur in our archive:

| Theory | SCO claim | Contemporaneous report | Modern assessment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Copyright** | Unix `abi`/`errno.h` etc. copied into Linux | [Copyright Trumps GPL](/articles/sco-claims-copyright-trumps-gpl-31357) + [Evidence Raises Questions](/articles/scos-evidence-raises-questions-about-case-31386) | No court found infringement; see [Groklaw archive](http://www.groklaw.net/) and [GNU GPL FAQ](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html) |
| **GPL invalid** | GPL is unenforceable, so Linux users need a SCO license | [Licensing Fight Further](/articles/sco-takes-linux-licensing-fight-further-31166) | [GPLv2 upheld in multiple jurisdictions](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/old-licenses/gpl-2.0.html) — contrary to SCO |
| **Contract (IBM)** | IBM breached AT&T Unix licenses, contributed code to Linux | [Yanks Second IBM License](/articles/sco-yanks-second-ibm-license-31344) + [Other Reality](/articles/sco-vs-ibm-the-other-reality-31479) | Case dismissed/settled without IBM liability; see [Heise timeline](https://www.heise.de/) |

<Callout type="warning" title="The tell">
  When the claimed code was finally shown — e.g., `errno.h` — reviewers per [our evidence piece](/articles/scos-evidence-raises-questions-about-case-31386) and [Groklaw](http://www.groklaw.net/) found it was either BSD-licensed or trivial. The *absence* of compelling evidence became the evidence.
</Callout>

## IBM's counter — patents as deterrence

[SCO's $1B suit](/articles/sco-takes-linux-licensing-fight-further-31166) became survivable when [IBM counterclaimed on patents](/articles/big-blue-hits-sco-with-patent-counterclaim-31287). The message, per our 2003 analysis, was "we can both sue." That mutual portfolio threat — now formalized in [OIN's patent non-aggression community](https://www.openinventionnetwork.com/) — is why [Linux Foundation's Open Source Guides](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/resources/open-source-guides) now treat patents as a first-class risk alongside copyright.

## Why it still matters for 2026 buyers

Three questions from 2003 are procurement questions today, answered per [Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/resources/open-source-guides) and [Harvard Law](https://hls.harvard.edu/):

1. **Is the GPL enforceable?** Yes — [GPLv2](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/old-licenses/gpl-2.0.html) has been upheld; SCO's theory failed. Verify via [GNU FAQ](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html).
2. **Who indemnifies me?** SCO tried to license *users* — a pattern now addressed by vendor indemnification and [OIN](https://www.openinventionnetwork.com/). Ask your distributor.
3. **Do patents threaten my use?** IBM's counter shows portfolios deter. Check [OIN membership](https://www.openinventionnetwork.com/) and request SBOM per [NTIA](https://www.ntia.gov/page/software-bill-materials).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Did SCO prove Linux copied Unix code?

No. When evidence was shown per [our report](/articles/scos-evidence-raises-questions-about-case-31386) and tracked on [Groklaw](http://www.groklaw.net/), it was not found to be infringing. Courts never ruled for SCO on the merits of copying.

### Was the GPL invalidated?

No — the opposite. [GPLv2](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/old-licenses/gpl-2.0.html) and later enforcement actions affirmed it. SCO's claim that GPL is unenforceable failed.

### What should enterprises check today because of SCO?

Three things per [Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/resources/open-source-guides): vendor indemnification, [OIN patent coverage](https://www.openinventionnetwork.com/), and SBOM per [NTIA](https://www.ntia.gov/page/software-bill-materials) — the checklist above.
