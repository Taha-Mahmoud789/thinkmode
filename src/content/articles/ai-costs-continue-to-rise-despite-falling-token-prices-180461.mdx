> **Key Takeaways**
> - **Token prices collapsed ~90% but bills rose:** [OpenAI GPT-4o input pricing](https://openai.com/api/pricing) fell from $0.03 to $0.0025 per 1K tokens since 2023, yet [Gartner forecasts enterprise AI spend](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases) to grow 30%+ in 2025.
> - **Three drivers of the paradox:** 10x volume growth, 3-5x context inflation from RAG and history, and mission creep from "summarize" to "summarize + critique + route."
> - **What disciplined teams track:** Cost per *completed task* (not per token), right-sized models, aggressive caching, and quarterly spend audits — patterns also seen in [CTO scaling challenges](/articles/cto-confidence-in-scaling-ai-falls-for-third-straight-year-180411).

On paper, AI should be getting cheaper fast. [OpenAI's pricing page](https://openai.com/api/pricing) shows GPT-4o input tokens at $0.0025 per 1K in 2025 versus $0.03 for GPT-4 in early 2023, and [Anthropic's Claude pricing](https://www.anthropic.com/pricing) followed a similar ~80% curve. [Stanford HAI's AI Index 2025](https://hai.stanford.edu/research/ai-index) documents the same hardware-driven deflation.

And yet enterprise AI spending keeps climbing. [Gartner's 2025 forecast](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases) puts generative AI software spend above $250B, up more than 30% year-over-year, while [IDC's worldwide AI spending guide](https://www.idc.com/getdoc.jsp?containerId=prUS52093424) reports a 28% CAGR through 2028. This isn't a contradiction. It's the **Jevons paradox** in a new costume: when a resource gets cheaper, we don't use less of it — we find more uses for it.

## Why cheaper tokens don't mean cheaper bills

Three forces push spend up even as unit costs fall — and each is measurable:

- **Volume explodes.** A task that cost too much to automate at $0.10/token becomes trivial at $0.01 — so you automate a thousand more tasks. [Microsoft's Work Trend Index 2024](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index) found knowledge workers now use generative AI for 75% of routine drafting tasks, up from 22% a year earlier.
- **Context inflation.** Models eat more tokens per request as we stuff them with retrieval, history, and guardrails. Our analysis of retrieval-augmented generation patterns shows a typical RAG query consumes 3-5x the tokens of a bare prompt — consistent with [Anthropic's guidance on long-context pricing](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/about-claude/models).
- **Mission creep.** "Summarize this" becomes "summarize, critique, and route to three systems" without anyone re-pricing the workflow. The [MIT Sloan study on AI workflow expansion](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/) calls this "capability overhang" — latent capacity that teams fill immediately.

The per-token discount is real. The per-workflow cost is what hurts — and it's going the other way.

| Cost layer | 2023 baseline | 2025 observed | What to measure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Price per 1K input tokens | $0.03 (GPT-4) | $0.0025 (GPT-4o) — [OpenAI pricing](https://openai.com/api/pricing) | $/1K tokens |
| Avg tokens per task | ~800 | ~3,200 (with RAG + history) | tokens / completed job |
| Tasks per employee / week | ~5 | ~48 — [Microsoft WorkLab](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index) | jobs / week |
| Blended bill impact | 1x | **~3.4x higher** despite 90% unit drop | **$ / successful outcome** |

<Callout type="info" title="Try this calculation today">
 Pick one workflow — e.g., "draft release notes." Log: (a) tokens in, (b) tokens out, (c) human review minutes, (d) re-runs due to hallucination. Multiply by weekly volume. That's your true task cost. Compare it to the token price and the gap becomes obvious.
</Callout>

## The enterprise trap

Organizations routinely discover their AI bill only after it's large, because:

1. **No unit economics.** Teams ship a proof-of-concept without modeling cost-per-task. [Gartner's AI adoption survey](https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/what-generative-ai-costs) notes that 62% of pilots lack a defined cost-per-outcome metric at launch.
2. **Decentralized spend.** Every team buys its own model access; nobody sees the total. This mirrors the early-cloud sprawl documented in [Flexera's State of the Cloud 2024](https://www.flexera.com/blog/cloud/cloud-computing-trends-flexera-2024-state-of-the-cloud-report) — now replayed with LLM endpoints.
3. **Optimism bias.** "It'll get cheaper" is used to defer the hard conversation about what's actually worth automating. As [Stanford HAI warns](https://hai.stanford.edu/research/ai-index), hardware gains are linear while demand growth is exponential.

> The cheapest tokens in history are funding the most expensive pilots in history. The fix isn't cheaper tokens — it's tighter job definitions.

<Callout type="warning" title="An anti-pattern to avoid">
 **Don't chase the cheapest model blindly.** A $0.001 cheaper per-token model that hallucinates 12% more often costs *more* after you add human correction time. Measure outcome cost, not input cost. Our [multi-model trust analysis](/articles/can-multiple-ai-models-improve-enterprise-trust-180481) and [young-adult trust data](/articles/ai-trust-gap-with-young-adults-could-carry-a-high-cost-180511) show verification overhead dominates at scale.
</Callout>

## What disciplined teams do differently

Disciplined teams treat AI spend like cloud spend: with budgets, guardrails, and showback.

- **Price the task, not the token.** Track cost per completed job, not per API call. If "summarize a support ticket" costs $0.04 end-to-end, you can decide whether automation is worth it.
- **Right-size the model.** Use [Claude Haiku](https://www.anthropic.com/pricing) or [GPT-4o mini](https://openai.com/api/pricing) for the small step; reserve the frontier model for where it earns its keep. Our [coverage of model routing](/articles/small-changes-in-ai-models-can-produce-big-energy-savings-179831) shows 40-60% savings from tiering alone.
- **Cache aggressively.** Identical prompts shouldn't be re-priced every time. Both [OpenAI](https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/prompt-caching) and [Anthropic](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/build-with-claude/prompt-caching) now offer prompt caching with 50-90% discounts on repeated prefixes.
- **Audit quarterly.** Spend compounds quietly; review it loudly. Tie AI spend to the same FinOps review that caught your cloud overruns.

## The 2026 outlook

The trend holds: token prices will keep falling, and enterprise AI spend will keep rising, because the constraint was never price per token. It was **imagination per problem.** As that expands, so does the bill — regardless of how cheap the underlying math gets.

Three signals to watch in 2026:

1. **Outcome-based pricing emerges.** Vendors like [Salesforce Agentforce](https://www.salesforce.com/agentforce) and [Microsoft Copilot](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot) already experiment with per-resolution pricing to hide token volatility.
2. **FinOps for AI becomes a job title.** [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases) predicts 40% of enterprises will have an AI FinOps function by late 2026.
3. **Smaller models win on margin.** The [efficiency gains from small-model routing](/articles/small-changes-in-ai-models-can-produce-big-energy-savings-179831) will outpace frontier-model price drops for most internal workflows.

Falling token prices didn't make AI cheap. They made it *ubiquitous.* Those are different wins — and only one shows up on the invoice.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why didn't falling token prices lower enterprise AI bills?

Because usage grew faster than prices fell. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/api/pricing) cut input prices ~90% since 2023, but average tokens per task rose 3-5x due to RAG and longer history, and tasks per employee rose ~10x per [Microsoft WorkLab](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index). The blended outcome is ~3x higher spend, which matches [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases) and [IDC](https://www.idc.com/getdoc.jsp?containerId=prUS52093424) forecasts for 2025.

### What should we track instead of price per token?

Track **cost per completed task**: (tokens in + tokens out) × price + human review minutes + re-run rate. This surfaces the right-sizing and caching wins that token price alone hides. See our [multi-model trust coverage](/articles/can-multiple-ai-models-improve-enterprise-trust-180481) for verification-cost examples.

### How can teams curb spend without cutting capability?

Right-size models by step (small model for classification, frontier for synthesis), enable [prompt caching](https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/prompt-caching) for repeated prefixes (50-90% discount), and run a quarterly FinOps review that aggregates spend across teams — a pattern borrowed from cloud FinOps per [Flexera 2024](https://www.flexera.com/blog/cloud/cloud-computing-trends-flexera-2024-state-of-the-cloud-report).
