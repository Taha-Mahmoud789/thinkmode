In a sweeping online [manifesto](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/08/the-future-is-for-everyone/) published Monday, Meta founder and CEO Mark Zuckerberg called for superintelligence to be widely available, arguing that putting advanced AI in the hands of individuals offers a better path than concentrating it among governments, companies, or a small group of experts.

"As intelligence becomes abundant, the most important question will be how we direct it," he wrote. "Some argue that superintelligence itself or a small set of experts who control it should decide what is best for humanity. We disagree."

Zuckerberg argued that distributing superintelligence broadly would extend the progression that put computing power and the internet into the hands of individuals, giving people greater ability to pursue their own goals and interests.

"Rather than centralizing superintelligence, we should distribute it widely and give every person the ability to direct it," he maintained. "This has the potential to begin a new era of personal empowerment where individuals can use this powerful new capability to reach their full potential, pursue their interests, and improve their lives and the world more than ever before."

### Breath of Fresh Air

Some technology observers welcomed Zuckerberg's optimism about AI. "Zuckerberg is providing one of the most optimistic perspectives on AI we’ve seen from a major tech CEO," observed Mark N. Vena, president and principal analyst at [SmartTech Research](https://www.smarttechresearch.net/ ""), a technology advisory firm in Las Vegas.

While acknowledging that Zuckerberg's optimism may be influenced by the amount of money Meta has invested in AI, Vena told TechNewsWorld "that optimism is a breath of fresh air compared to the constant apocalypse narrative around AI."

"But the real test will not be whether Meta can democratize intelligence, but whether society can democratize the economic value intelligence creates," he added.

The essay is an optimistic counterweight to AI doom, but it is ultimately a corporate manifesto for Wall Street to believe in Meta's stock, maintained Rich Pleeth, co-founder and CEO of [Finmile](https://finmile.co/ ""), an AI-powered logistics software company in London.

"Zuckerberg’s central view that advanced AI should expand individual capability rather than be controlled exclusively by a handful of governments and corporations is certainly interesting, but distributing powerful technology is not, by itself, a safety strategy," he told TechNewsWorld.

"Access has to be accompanied by accountability, transparency, and meaningful controls over how these systems can act," he said.

### Business-Shaped Philosophy

Pleeth also noted that Zuckerberg's position in his essay aligns with Meta’s commercial strategy of developing open-source AI models. "Open-weight models weaken competitors' ability to charge a premium for proprietary intelligence and allow Meta to compete through distribution and its enormous consumer platforms," he explained.

Vena agreed. "Zuckerberg has turned openness into a philosophical argument and a competitive weapon against the companies that believe controlling their most powerful models creates a better business and safety model," he said.

"While Meta wants to become the tools people and companies build around, that doesn't make Mark wrong," added Russell Twilligear, head of AI research and development at [BlogBuster](https://www.blogbuster.so/ ""), an AI-powered blogging platform, in Norway.

"You can have a business reason for taking a position but still be right at the same time," he told TechNewsWorld. "This is one of those cases."

"I've known Mark for a long time now," he said. "What he's saying here is a real vision, and it's a business case for Meta. These two things can be true at the same time."

[Shama Hyder](https://shamahyder.com/rewired/ ""), founder of Rewired, an AI fluency program for executives, in Miami, explained that every CEO manifesto is also a positioning document.

"Meta chose open weights, so its philosophy of the future conveniently makes open weights the moral high ground," she told TechNewsWorld. "That doesn't make the argument wrong."

"It does mean you should read it the way you'd read any strategy memo," she added. "The worldview follows the business model at least as much as the business model follows the worldview."

### Access Not Necessarily Empowerment

David Gaidukovych, head of marketing for [Regenics](https://regenics.com/ ""), a health care company in Tarzana, Calif., agreed with Zuckerberg's goal of broadly distributing AI capabilities but argued that access and empowerment are not the same thing.

"Access can be democratized faster than advantage can be democratized," he told TechNewsWorld.

"Giving everyone access to a powerful AI agent does not mean everyone will benefit equally from it," he said. "I would describe this emerging challenge as the AI advantage gap — the difference between having access to AI and having the ability to transform AI into measurable economic value."

He explained that converting AI into value means integrating AI into real workflows, such as improving decision-making, creating new products, building more efficient businesses, and generating measurable results.

[![Discover how NiCE AI agents empower enterprises](https://www.ectnews.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2026/07/nice-q326-970x250-cogsoles.jpg)](https://www.technewsworld.com/story/mark-zuckerberg-makes-the-case-for-superintelligence-for-everyone-180491.html)

"Two people or companies may have access to the same AI tools, but the outcomes can be completely different," he continued. "One may use AI simply to complete tasks faster, while another may use it to redesign operations, create new products, improve customer experiences, or build entirely new business models."

"The next digital divide may not be between people who have AI and people who do not," he noted. "It may be between those who can convert AI into better decisions, products and businesses, and those who use the same technology mainly as a convenience."

"From an economic and marketing perspective, the key question is not only who gets access to AI, but who learns how to create value with it," he added.

### No Progress Without Pain

In his essay, Zuckerberg noted that humanity has witnessed many transformative advances. "Each time there is fear that people will be left behind," he wrote. "But each time humanity has come out with more people sharing greater prosperity, health, and freedom."

"We believe this will be true with AI as well, and the abundance of the future can be shared by everyone," he continued. "We also believe that the values that got us to this point — like liberty, open inquiry, free enterprise, and equal opportunity — are also the right values to build a positive future."

While Zuckerberg's reasoning is directionally true, it hides the part that hurts: the destination is better than the transition, Hyder contended.

"The transition is where people lose careers, towns and identities, and transitions are measured in decades," she explained. "Farmers didn't become factory workers in a news cycle."

"What's different this time is speed: the technology is compounding faster than human behavior, skills, and institutions can adapt, and that adaptation gap is where the pain lives," she continued. "Optimism about the destination is fine. The pace of the transition is what leaders should be planning for."

Zuckerberg does propose safeguards beyond broad distribution, including government-industry cooperation, independent oversight of model releases, and checks and balances among competing AI systems.

Zuckerberg's essay is a bet that distribution solves alignment, safety, and jobs all at once, Hyder argued. "Maybe," she said, "but the whole vision rests on a variable he spends almost no time on: human behavior."

"Adoption, trust and fluency will decide whether personal superintelligence empowers a billion people or just automates their attention," she reasoned. "AI is the accelerant. Human behavior is the signal. The leaders who plan for the human side of this transition are the ones who'll actually get the future Zuckerberg is describing."
